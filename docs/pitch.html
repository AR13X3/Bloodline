Why now

1 July 2026

Patients can see results first

Pathology and imaging reports now go to My Health Record by default, mostly without the old 7-day delay.7 A patient may see a result before anyone at the practice has acted on it.

26 August 2026

Follow-up is an accreditation standard

The RACGP’s new 6th edition Standards keep a dedicated criterion for follow-up systems, CG6.8,9 Accreditation is a condition of PIP QI payments.10

Late 2025

Labs have started texting doctors

Australian Clinical Labs texts doctors about high-risk results and asks them to confirm, citing the Medical Board’s expectations.11 It covers one lab, judged by that lab’s own criteria.

2019 → 2024

Fewer GPs for each patient

Full-time equivalent GPs per 100,000 people fell from 117 to 113.2 Each GP covers more patients, and more results.

Halo Connect

One integration reaches most practices

Halo Connect is the only connection method for Bp Omni, and Bp Premier integrations are moving to it.31,32 Best Practice runs in 75.9% of practices.4

AU Core FHIR

Result data is standardising

Australia’s core FHIR standard now defines a profile for pathology results.36 Structured values become the main path, with OCR as the fallback.

Why no one has built this yet

We found adjacent products, not a direct competitor (see Competitors). These are the likely reasons.

  • Access is gated

    Results sit inside practice software. Best Practice grants partner access only after a stringent review,30 Halo Connect supports only approved partners,33 and MedicalDirector has no public developer portal.34

  • Each player sees part of the problem

    A lab sees only its own results.11 Patient-messaging tools act after the GP has decided.17 The practice software shows every result, but only when the GP opens the inbox.13

  • Regulation and liability put builders off Our view

    Software that flags results may be a medical device, and the exemption for decision-support software needs specialist advice.35 A product that promises alerts also takes on the risk of missing one.

  • The market looked small to large vendors Our view

    About $29.1M a year across every Australian GP is too small for large software companies to prioritise. That leaves room for a focused product.

  • The single integration route is new

    With Halo Connect, integrators no longer build and support their own agent in every practice.42 One partnership now reaches most of the market.

Assumptions

  • Ramp (hypothesis). Practices live at the end of each year are 1%, 5%, 20%, 50% and 100% of the Year-5 count, net of churn. Year 1 is a paid NSW pilot. Year 2 adds NSW practices through PHNs and medical groups. Year 3 goes national through the Best Practice partner route. Years 4 and 5 add MedicalDirector practices.
  • Lean plan (hypothesis). $60 per GP (PRD H1). 2,400 practices by Year 5, the same reach as the ambitious scenario, which depends on PHN and medical-group deals that sign many practices at once. The core team grows to about 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 people. One support person covers 200 practices, assuming practices set BloodLine up themselves: Halo Connect monitors the connection and supports practices, so we don’t run a help desk for it.42
  • Other scenarios use a core team of 10 by Year 5 and one support person per 150 practices.
  • Start. Year 1 begins with the first paying practice, after 30 practice interviews, regulatory advice and a shadow pilot (PRD §14.7).
  • Revenue in a year averages the practices live at its start and end. ARR counts the practices live at year end.
  • People are the core team plus the customer team. Overheads and marketing add 25% of people costs (estimate).
  • Compliance, legal and insurance cost $100–120k a year (estimate): regulatory and legal advice, a privacy impact assessment, a yearly penetration test ($15–40k for a SaaS platform40), ISO 27001 from Year 2 ($15–80k+41) and insurance.
  • Funding needed is the largest cumulative loss over the five years. A “+” means the company still loses money in Year 5. A real raise adds a buffer on top.
  • Not included: GST, tax, R&D tax incentives, grants, price rises and expansion markets.

What a practice gets back

In the first year, a practice’s return is safety and accreditation, not billing. Most follow-up consultations would happen anyway through the normal inbox. BloodLine only adds the ones for results that would otherwise have been missed, and we don’t yet know how many that is. The shadow pilot measures it before we claim it.

  • Evidence for the accreditation follow-up criterion9, which PIP QI payments depend on.10
  • Fewer missed results, and fewer of the complaints that follow them.16
  • Less staff time spent chasing results (to measure in the pilot).

What practices use today

Included in practice software

The practice software inbox

Best Practice (75.9% of practices) and MedicalDirector (21.3%)4 put results in the GP’s inbox. The GP marks an action, a follow-up inbox lists recalls, and Bp Comms can text patients.13,14,15

The GP has to open the inbox. We found no push to the GP’s phone, no per-patient thresholds and no handover when the GP is away.

Run by one lab

Lab alerts

Australian Clinical Labs’ eResults sends alerts for urgent results by push notification, SMS and email. High-risk results are texted, and the doctor replies to confirm.11,12

One lab’s results, judged by that lab’s criteria. We found no equivalent from Sonic or Healius.

Manual

Practice processes

Registers, staff checks and reminders on the patient file, as the RACGP standards require.9

Depends on people remembering. Informal processes have led to missed results and Ahpra complaints.16

Our position. We found no Australian product that does all of these together: checks every result from every lab as it arrives, against the printed range and the GP’s own rules; pushes to the GP’s phone; hands over to a covering colleague; and says when a result couldn’t be checked.

Competitive risks

  • Best Practice could add alerts to its own inbox. It also controls our integration route through Halo Connect, so we aim to be a partner, not a rival.
  • Lab-led alerts like Australian Clinical Labs’ could spread to Sonic and Healius.
  • HotDoc and Cubiko already sell to most of our buyers and could add a similar feature.
  • AI inbox tools such as MediRecords and Yes AI are moving from filing results to flagging them.
